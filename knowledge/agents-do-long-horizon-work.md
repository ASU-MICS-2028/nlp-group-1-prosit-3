# Agents became possible once models could work for 15 to 30 minutes on one task without going off track

People wanted AI that "accomplishes work", not just answers. Early coding tools were autocomplete. Agents (Cursor, Claude Code, Codex; GitHub Copilot started it) became possible once models could work over a **long horizon**, 15 to 30 minutes on one task, building and then testing a whole codebase. The instructor said most AI revenue still comes from coding.

**Personal agents** (he credited OpenClaw, around the end of 2025) go further than a chatbot: the agent has its own computer, works proactively, asks when it needs something and reports back, like an intern. A student recalled an agent that kept deleting emails after being told to stop; check before citing.

**Example (Lecture 9 lab):** send receipts only; the agent logs them in a sheet, files them, answers "how much have I spent?" and builds a summary deck.

**For Prosit 3:** not core; it shows why human control matters once AI acts on its own.
